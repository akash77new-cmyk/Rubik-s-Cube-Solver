# Notation: how moves are written

The letters, primes and doubles the app uses for every move.

## One letter per side

Each side has a letter: U (up, the top), D (down, the bottom), L (left), R (right),
F (front, facing you) and B (back). A move turns one outer layer by a quarter turn.

## Clockwise, primes and doubles

A letter alone, like R, means turn that side a quarter turn clockwise, as if you were looking
straight at that side. A prime, like R' (said "R prime"), means anticlockwise. A 2, like R2,
means a half turn; its direction does not matter.

## The tricky sides: B, L and D

Clockwise is always judged by looking at the side itself. So B turns the back layer clockwise
as seen from behind the cube, which looks anticlockwise from the front. The same goes for L and
D. This is the most common reason a solution goes wrong. The app's plain-English instructions
say which way to turn as seen from where you stand, for example "Turn the top layer to the left."

## Reading a sequence

A sequence like R U R' U' is done left to right: R, then U, then R', then U'. To undo a
sequence, do it backwards with every turn reversed: the inverse of R U is U' R'.
